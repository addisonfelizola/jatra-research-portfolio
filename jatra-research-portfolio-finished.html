Eight chapters - Patan, Nepal, 2081

KARUNAMAYA

The Rato Macchindranath Rath Yatra of Patan, Nepal

Every spring, a city prays for rain.

↓

The tangible impacts of a changing climate have become more pronounced in recent years, and the implications for the Himalaya are significant: more frequent and intense droughts, weather and agricultural anomalies, and consequences for water, food, and livelihood security. Mountain communities are particularly vulnerable, dependent as they are on rapidly melting glaciers for drinking water and agriculture.

This portfolio follows one answer that is already more than a thousand years old. Among the cultural practices of the Newa, the Indigenous people of the Kathmandu Valley, is the Machchhindranath Jatra: a months-long annual festival celebrated to thwart catastrophic drought and famine, and a living record of how a community organises itself to survive catastrophe, together.

The photographs were taken by the author during the Jatra of Nepali year 2081 (2024) in Patan, also known as Lalitpur. They are held in three public albums on Facebook; each frame in this portfolio carries the description recorded during the review of those albums and, where one was recorded, links to the photograph itself. A small number of frames have no individual link recorded, and are marked as held in the album.

“How does the Machchhindranath Jatra teach us to be resilient in the face of a changing climate?” The research question - Shangrila Joshi, PhD
